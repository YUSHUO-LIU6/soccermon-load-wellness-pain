## 3. Pre-specified analysis strategy

- **Primary analysis: complete-case.** The main onset-day models use player-days with the relevant lagged wellness exposure observed. This is the transparent reference analysis; the complete-case fraction and the factors predicting observation (Section 2) are reported alongside it.
- **Key sensitivity analysis: IPW.** Observation models from Section 2 provide p_hat for the day each lag-1 exposure was recorded; stabilized weights sw = P_marginal(observed)/p_hat, truncated at the within-variable 1st/99th percentiles, are applied to the complete-case sample. Diagnostics in `ipw_diagnostics.csv` / `ipw_effective_sample_size.csv`:

| Variable | CC n | Max sw (trunc) | ESS (trunc) | ESS/n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fatigue | 16757 | 10.9 (6.3) | 6774 | 0.40 |
| readiness | 16761 | 10.9 (6.3) | 6753 | 0.40 |
| soreness | 16762 | 10.9 (6.3) | 6764 | 0.40 |

- **Multilevel multiple imputation: not the primary analysis.** Feasibility assessment: (i) only 42 onset events and 15 event players — an imputation model would have to preserve the exposure–event relationship with extremely sparse outcome information; (ii) exposures enter as lag-1 and rolling 3-day summaries, so imputation must respect the longitudinal lag structure (a joint multilevel model of 7 correlated wellness scales x ~730 days x 50 players, with within-player standardization), which is computationally and methodologically fragile in standard MI software; (iii) reporting is demonstrably non-random and plausibly MNAR, so MAR-based MI offers no clear advantage over the transparent complete-case + IPW combination while adding substantial modelling assumptions. Multilevel MI is therefore judged **not reasonable** as the primary approach for this revision.
- **MNAR tipping-point sensitivity: not pursued quantitatively.** A formal delta-adjustment tipping-point analysis would require specifying a sensitivity parameter for the unobservable contrast between reporters and non-reporters on 1–5/1–10 ordinal scales, mapped through lag-1 and 3-day averaged exposures into a survival model with 42 events; the resulting parameter space cannot be credibly bounded from these data. Instead we (a) report exactly which observed factors predict reporting (Section 2), and (b) present the IPW sensitivity analysis, and state the residual MNAR risk as a limitation.

## 4. Required wording

Manuscript and response letter must **not** claim that missing-data bias was "eliminated", "corrected", or "removed". Approved phrasing: "IPW sensitivity analyses assess the robustness of the complete-case estimates to non-random observation; residual bias due to missing-not-at-random reporting cannot be excluded."
